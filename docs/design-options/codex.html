Field notes from the orchard

on the weather, the wasps, and what the espalier is doing this week │ drafted │ last edit 06:54
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The mornings have turned colder than they should be for May, and the cordons along the south wall are flowering anyway, as if to argue with the thermometer. I went out at half-six to see whether the frost had taken the blossom — it had not — but the bees were absent and the wasps were already on the cherries, which is earlier than I have ever recorded them.

I have begun to suspect that the small notebook from 2023 — the brown one — was right about something I had dismissed: the rosemary planted along the path is doing more for the pollinator count than the lavender ever did. The lavender is theatrical; the rosemary is reliable.

What the trees showed

The Reinette is two weeks ahead. The Cox is on schedule. The two Bramleys, planted on the same day from the same nursery, are not even pretending to agree with each other anymore.

  • Reinette — full blossom, no frost damage, wasps already present.
  • Cox — green tip, no flower, healthy.
  • Bramley A — full blossom, vigorous.
  • Bramley B — half blossom, suspected canker on south leader.
A garden is the only mirror in which you can watch yourself become slower. J. Berger, from memory

Notes on pruning

I keep meaning to write down the rule that the old man down the lane gave me: never cut what you cannot name. He meant the small wood — the spurs and the laterals — but I think he meant it more widely than that, and I have been trying to apply it to other things lately.

The rule does not survive contact with brambles. Brambles are an exception to every rule, including the rule that there are no exceptions.

A reminder for next year

The summer prune on the espaliered pears should be done the week of St Swithin's, not before, and the tools should be sharpened the night before — not the morning of, which is what I always do, and then I am cross with the steel for the whole hour.
