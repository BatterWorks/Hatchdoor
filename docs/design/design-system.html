Callout accents

One hue per meaning, not one per kind name. A callout's spine, label, and lead rule are all this colour and its surface tint is mixed from it, so a kind needs a single value and nothing else. Kinds that mean the same thing share an accent; kinds that mean different things never do.

These are mixed like press inks, not picked as status colours. Forge owns an unusually narrow band: every other chromatic token in this document sits between 35° and 95° in OKLCh, running from --hot through ochre to cream. Telling nine kinds apart needs more hue spread than that wedge holds — hue is the only discriminator that survives at a 0.7rem label — so the hues falling outside it earn their place by giving up chroma. A pressman's blue on cream is a dusty slate, never a process blue. Hence --co-info at C 0.048 against oxblood's 0.115: the further a hue sits from the house wedge, the greyer it has to be. Danger keeps the most chroma and sits on --hot's own hue, so it stays the loudest thing a note can say.

All nine share one lightness per theme, so no kind's label outweighs another's. Light values below; dark holds the same hues and the same chroma ranking at L 0.720.

§05

Sidebar (Explorer)

Four zones: the Scope zone (#138), a fixed rail of whole-vault destinations, the scrolling note navigation, and the create action pinned at the bottom. Only the third scrolls — scope, rail and footer never move. The rail lives inside the sidebar rather than the topbar on purpose: the topbar’s four mobile slots are a hard budget, and the drawer sits outside it. The Scope zone is the one conditional zone: absent at one enabled Vault and absent below 920px, where the topbar’s scope row carries it instead (§30).

The Scope zone takes --paper, one step lighter than the sidebar’s own --bg. It is the only zone with a ground of its own, and it earns one: pinned above a scrolling list, it needs to read as a fixed shelf rather than as the first few rows of the navigation that moves beneath it. The token inverts correctly — --paper is the lighter neighbour of --bg in both themes — so the shelf lifts in light and in dark without a second declaration. The rail below it stays on the pane ground; two stacked shelves would be a stripe, not a hierarchy.

Scope rows are set one step tighter than the tree below them: 0.82rem against the tree’s own size, 0.32rem 0.35rem padding, 0.5rem gap. The zone is a register of what exists, read once and then ignored; the tree is the thing under the cursor all day. Sizing them alike made the sidebar open with two equal claims on attention. Rows keep --font-display with its wdth/wght axes rather than dropping to the sans face — a Vault name is a name, and the display face is what names are set in everywhere else in this app.

Collapsed, the zone is head plus viewing line and nothing else — no residual gap where the rows were. .side-head carries a 0.7rem bottom margin for its ordinary use in the tree, and is declared after .scope-zone-head at equal specificity, so the collapsed head silently inherits a margin under nothing unless the override is parent-scoped as .scope-zone .scope-zone-head. Any future rule on this head needs the same scoping or it will not apply at all.

Recency panels align to the Scope zone, not to the tree. RECENTLY VIEWED and CHANGED ON DISK are lists Hatchdoor keeps, the same kind of thing SCOPE is, so they sit at the zone’s outer edge — negative margins cancelling .explorer-nav’s 1rem inset back to the zone’s 0.5rem, giving one shared left edge and one rule width. The Vault accordion keeps the deeper inset. Indent is the signal: headings this app owns at the outer edge, the Vaults you named one step inside. Their heads also drop .side-head’s 0.7rem to 0.35rem — that margin is sized for the tree’s taller sections, and a head sitting straight onto a five-row list does not need it.

Folders use a CSS-only chevron caret that rotates open. Notes show a mono index — that is what tells note rows apart from folder rows without changing size or weight. Active note: hot-orange left rail + hot-soft background + bolded, and it is canonical in the tree only. Applying it in the recency lists too is what made one note light up in three places at once.

The index is centred optically, not geometrically. It is always lining digits — ascent only, no descender — while the title beside it hangs about two pixels below their shared baseline. align-items: center lines the two boxes up exactly and still leaves the digits reading high by half that descender, so .idx takes a translateY(0.1em) correction, in em so it survives a resize. The residual is content-dependent — a title full of descenders sits lower than an all-caps one — so the target is the face’s typical profile, never any single string’s ink.

Depth is carried by two devices, and both descend. Size separates a Vault from its contents: the accordion head is the only row in the tree set above reading size, at 0.95rem. Folders and notes both stay at 0.9rem and separate by weight and marker instead — wght 500 with a caret against wght 400 with the mono index — because they are what you actually read, and buying a third size step by shrinking them would cost legibility to say something weight already says. Carets shrink the same way: 6×10 at a Vault head, 6×6 at a folder. A child marker larger than its parent’s reads as the child outranking it, which is what an 8×8 folder caret under a 5×8 head was doing.

Row rhythm descends with depth too: 26px between Vault heads, 24.5px between folders, 23.9px between notes. Pitch alone is not what the eye reads — before this the three were within 2px of each other and the tree still felt slack, because a head fills its box with large bold type while a note puts small light type in the same box, and the emptier box reads as looser. Rows are sized by the ratio of ink to box, not by matching numbers.

Section heads take a label, a rule, and a mono count. A collapsible section adds the same caret the folder rows use, so a foldable thing reads the same way wherever it appears. Section numerals are optional — use them only where the order carries meaning.

Prose & inline elements

Reading measure: uncapped. This document previously specified max-width: 36rem on the note body; the app has always rendered full width, about 100 characters at 1440px. Capping was considered and rejected. Nearly every non-prose block here is a bordered box — .table-wrap, .callout, .code-block — so a narrow text column beside full-width boxes reads as broken alignment rather than as hierarchy, and capping every block uniformly instead pushes tables and diagrams into horizontal scrolling much sooner. §13

Callouts

A marked aside, not a coloured box. Colour appears in three thin places: the spine at the left edge, the kind label, and the hairline lead rule that carries the label to the right edge and closes the top of the block, which is why a callout has no top, right, or bottom border. The surface is tinted 12% from the same accent — enough that the hue is legible at a glance, far short of a fill that competes with the prose it sits in. Body text is italic serif; body ink stays --ink-soft in every kind, because accent-coloured prose reads as an alert rather than as a note.

Each kind sets one custom property, --callout-accent, and inherits everything else. An unrecognised kind keeps the neutral --muted mark rather than borrowing --hot, which would make an unknown callout the loudest thing on the page. Tints mix in oklab: it holds the hue on the paper's warm axis in light and lifts it evenly off near-black in dark, where sRGB greys out at these percentages.

Collapsible

A trailing + or - on the marker ([!tip]-) makes the callout a <details> with .callout-collapsible, and the title becomes its <summary>; + starts open, - starts closed. Flex layout drops the UA disclosure triangle, so the state is stated by a caret drawn in the accent, which turns on open and holds still under prefers-reduced-motion. Collapsed, a callout is a single labelled rule — it costs one line until it is wanted.

Every marker the renderer accepts, and the accent it resolves to. Aliases within a row are the same colour by design: they mean the same thing. An unlisted marker is not an error — it renders as a callout with the neutral --muted mark and its own name as the label.

The dialog’s own filter — never the browsing scope

The sidebar decides what you are browsing; the dialog decides what you are looking at right now. Selecting a facet (or the phone’s Scope field) narrows only the results in view, changes no ranking, and dies the moment the dialog closes. No future surface may adopt either as a scope control (#119).

Desktop — the facet rail

A narrow column beside the results: every Vault the search reached, each ending in #116’s own count-or-condition slot. All results is the permanent widening row. A Vault that answered with nothing reads 0, inert; a Vault that did not answer keeps its row, reading no answer in error ink, aria-disabled rather than removed. Order is Vault-management order, never re-sorted by count or condition (#117). Absent when scope is narrowed or at one enabled Vault — there is nothing to filter across, and the Scope zone is already on screen behind the overlay.

§20

Inline editing

Clicking a block swaps it for a textarea holding that block’s own markdown. The affordance is a rule in the left gutter, not a fill and not a box: it reuses the device the list bullet already uses, so it reads as content rather than as a control, and it costs no horizontal space, so nothing reflows.

It is drawn with ::after because ::before is already the list marker, and .editable-block is the rendered element itself. Wrapping blocks in a div would sit between a ul and its li children and break every marker rule.

Resting: no mark at all, so a note reads as a document.

Hovered: a 1px hot hairline at 45% in the gutter.

Active: the same device at 2px and full weight. No ring, no fill, so it does not fight the borders callouts and tables already carry.

.editable-block.editable-block:hover.editable-block.is-active--edit-gutter

The gutter is borrowed, not owned

The note body sits flush against its container. The space the rule occupies is .note-pane’s padding, which is 56px on desktop and 16px on a phone, and that element is overflow-x: hidden. Clipping happens at the padding box, so a negative-margin rule survives, but the offset has to be a token rather than a constant.

Block input: match the metrics, never the transforms

The input inherits family, size, weight, line-height and colour from the block it replaces, and explicitly resets text-transform. Three rendered styles in this system uppercase their text — h1, h3, h6, callout titles and table headers — and an input that inherited that would show the typist a different string than the one being written to the file.

The line’s markdown prefix is measured and hung into the gutter, so the visible text does not move on entry. A fixed hang cannot work: - is about 9px while the bullet it replaces is inset 22.4px, and the width varies per prefix. On a phone the hang is clamped to the pane’s 16px, so a long prefix shifts slightly rather than being clipped.

Touch

A tap reads; a double tap (400ms, within 30px) enters a block. Tap-to-edit would raise the keyboard on every stray touch, and reading is the dominant mode on a phone. This was a 500ms long press until a real phone showed it racing the OS text-selection gesture: nothing sets user-select on the note body, so holding raised selection handles and the copy bar against the block opening. A double tap makes no contact with that gesture, and the window can be generous because a single tap on prose has no action to delay.

touch-action: manipulation on the block is load-bearing rather than polish. The viewport sets no maximum-scale, so without it a double tap zooms the page instead of opening the block; it drops double-tap zoom while leaving panning and pinch-zoom intact. Only a real touch pointer arms the two-tap requirement, so a screen reader activating the focused block still enters on one activation.

The gutter rule is drawn persistently at low opacity on coarse pointers, but it is ambient signal, not instruction: a hairline says something is here and cannot say what gesture reaches it. A one-time notice, Double-tap a line to edit it., carries the teaching. It retires on the first landed edit rather than on entry, so an accidental double tap does not count as having taught anything.

It borrows the write notice's shell and its × rather than being silently its own dismiss target. On touch, cursor: pointer is the only signal a self-dismissing line has, and there is no cursor: a notice that can be dismissed has to look like one. The × takes a 44px target here, because this notice only ever meets a finger, while the shared device keeps its own size where it is also a mouse target.

Island caption / enclosure

Under all scope with more than one participating Vault, the knowledge graph (§27's slot vocabulary reused, not a new one) becomes one field: every Vault's component is laid out on its own and placed as a labelled island in a single shared canvas, with one zoom and one pan across all of them (#118, implemented by #143). Both primitives here are canvas furniture — drawn with the graph's own <canvas> 2D context, not a DOM element — so this demo approximates the draw with inline SVG rather than reusing a real component class. The shape never changes with Vault count: at eight Vaults the answer is to zoom, not for the page to become something else. Narrowing to one Vault removes every caption and enclosure and restores today's single-Vault graph unchanged.

The count line names its unit here — 42 notes, not the bare 42 §27 uses in the sidebar. The slot vocabulary is the same, but the sidebar figure sits on a row that already says what it counts, and this one floats in open canvas with nothing beside it. A caption is read alone, so it carries its own unit; the condition word still replaces the line outright, and gets no unit because a word is not a quantity.

Each column is as wide as its own widest island, each row as tall as its own tallest — never one spacing derived from the largest Vault anywhere. That single figure failed in both directions at once: a 49-note Vault outgrew the shared cell and overlapped its neighbours by up to 120px, while 2-note Vaults sat in cells a third wider than they needed. Grid order is untouched by this — it is still Vault-management order — only the cell sizes vary. Because an island's true radius does not exist until the layout settles, the packing estimates it from node count and deliberately over-estimates: spare space inside a cell costs only zoom, since the view fits the whole field, whereas an underestimate overlaps two Vaults and makes both illegible.

The landing view fits the whole field, captions included — a caption clipped at the viewport edge is exactly as unreadable as a missing island. The fit runs on every frame while the layout settles rather than once at the end: alphaDecay(0.02) leaves the simulation warm for about six seconds, and a view that jumps that long after you arrive reads as the page glitching. It stops the moment the layout cools or the reader pans, zooms or touches the canvas — an auto-fit that fights a deliberate pan is worse than none. Zoom is capped at the single-graph landing scale, so a lone small Vault never arrives magnified.

§29

Vault accordion

Under all scope the explorer is an accordion of per-Vault sections with exactly one Vault unfolded at a time. Every Vault keeps a permanent one-line head; only the unfolded one shows a tree, so the explorer stays one tree plus N one-line heads whatever N is — bounded height is the whole argument. The head is the documented section head (§05) with the Vault name in the label slot and §27's own count-or-condition slot trailing; unfolded takes the active treatment, folded is plain. It is one click target with one job — unfold this Vault. It does not narrow scope. Clicking the unfolded head folds it, leaving nothing unfolded — the same plain list of names this section already documents for an instance with no history, and the only way back to it by hand. A head that ignores every click after the first reads as broken.

The Vault name keeps the capitalisation it was given. It is the operator’s own text, not a label this app wrote, so the head overrides .side-label’s uppercase and tracking — the same reasoning §20 applies to the inline editor, where an inherited text-transform would show the typist a different string than the one on disk. Uppercasing it also collapsed the one signal separating these heads from SCOPE and RECENTLY VIEWED above them: caps mean a heading Hatchdoor owns, sentence case means your data. The Scope zone and the accordion name the same Vaults, and must name them identically.

The gap belongs to the group, not to every head. An unfolded head owns the tree directly beneath it and closes up to 0.2rem; the separating 0.5rem moves below the tree, so one Vault is held off the next by the same distance whether the group is a single line or a whole tree. Carried under every head instead, it put a folder further from its own Vault than from the following one — proximity claiming the opposite of the nesting. The closing margin is scoped .explorer-nav > .tree.root-tree so it reaches accordion trees only and never the recency lists, whose own root tree is nested inside its panel.

Defaults and memory. Landing with a note open unfolds the Vault holding that note. Landing with none unfolds the last one, persisted per browser. Neither, and nothing is unfolded — a plain list of Vault names is the honest picture of an instance with no history. The landing Vault is read directly off the URL (a direct link to a note) or the stored last-note entry (landing at the root), rather than waited for from the open note's own content fetch, so the accordion's first paint never races that fetch or App.tsx's own last-note redirect. Folder open state is remembered per Vault, namespaced under the shared expandedFolders record so two Vaults' identically-named folders (two Journals) never share open/closed state; narrowed-scope browsing keeps today's unnamespaced keys untouched.

Narrowing to one Vault deletes the accordion entirely: the heads disappear and the single Notes head (§05) returns with §27's count-or-condition slot in place of its plain count, so a narrowed view — and a single-Vault instance, which never sees the accordion at all — is byte-identical to a single-Vault Hatchdoor. Widening restores the accordion with the Vault just left unfolded: the narrowed scope and the accordion's own memory are the same value, so leaving a Vault and returning to all reopens exactly where you were.

Truncation priority is fixed, not proportional. Under width pressure, in order: the rule collapses first (a flex-shrink two orders of magnitude past the name's absorbs nearly all the shrinkage before the name loses a pixel), then the Vault name ellipsizes, and the status word never truncates (flex-shrink: 0) — the same "condition over path" priority the sidebar's own slot already carries. Asserted at the narrowest supported phone width with a long Vault name.
